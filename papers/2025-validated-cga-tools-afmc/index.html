Systematic Application of Validated Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment Tools in a Tertiary Military Hospital: A Prospective Observational Study

Prospective observational study evaluating the systematic use of CGA tools (MMSE, MoCA, GDS, Barthel Index, FRAX, Fried Frailty Criteria) for holistic patient evaluation and individualized care planning at AFMC Pune.

Published 1 June 2025
GeriatricsCGAFrailtyCognitive AssessmentMilitary MedicineResearch

Abstract

See description above. Full manuscript in preparation.

Background

Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment (CGA) is the cornerstone of geriatric medicine practice globally. The combination of cognitive screening (MMSE, MoCA), mood assessment (GDS-15), functional evaluation (Barthel Index), fracture risk estimation (FRAX), and frailty staging (Fried Frailty Criteria) provides a holistic, individualized picture of the older patient that single-domain assessments cannot replicate.

Despite strong evidence for CGA’s clinical and health-economic benefits — shorter hospital stays, reduced readmissions, improved discharge destination — its implementation in Indian tertiary hospitals has been inconsistent, particularly in the military medical ecosystem.

This study documents our experience with systematic, protocol-driven CGA application in the Geriatrics Department at AFMC Pune.

Preliminary Findings

Over the study period, systematic CGA at admission enabled:

  • Identification of undiagnosed mild cognitive impairment in a significant proportion of patients admitted for non-cognitive primary complaints
  • Detection of frailty (Fried score ≥ 3) in a substantial proportion of patients, influencing surgical risk stratification and rehabilitation goals
  • FRAX-guided osteoporosis therapy initiation in patients with no prior bone health evaluation
  • Family counselling and Advance Care Planning conversations triggered by GDS-15-identified depression and Barthel-evidenced functional decline

Clinical Implications

The data support routine CGA as a standard of care rather than a specialist add-on. Embedding CGA at the point of geriatric ward admission — not as a consultant request — changes outcomes.
